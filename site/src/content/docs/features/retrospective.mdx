A retrospective in Parley is not a core session kind. It is a plugin: the
package under `plugins/retrospective/` declares the kind, the Wasm guest stores
the board, and the iframe UI fills the room. Enabling the install is what puts
**Retrospective** on the create dialog; there is no `KindDef` for it in the
shipped frontend.

## What the room does

- Three columns to start: went well, to improve, puzzles.
- Anyone can add a card. The author is stored, and **hidden from the wire
  state until the facilitator reveals**.
- Cards in the same column can be grouped.
- Each person may put one dot on a card (a second click does not stack).
- Action items are a list on the same board. An action with no owner typed
  reads **Unassigned**; anyone can name an owner later, or delete the action,
  from its menu.
- **A note can be deleted** by whoever wrote it, or by the facilitator, from
  the note's menu, after a confirmation. Its votes, stamps and links go with
  it. Before the reveal the board does not know whose a note is, so Delete is
  offered to everyone and the server answers; the removal is announced as "A
  note was removed", never as whose.
- Revealing authors asks for a second confirmation. Once revealed, each card
  shows its author's name. The facilitator can **hide authors again** in one
  press: the wire state stops carrying them, though anyone who was looking has
  already seen them. Only the facilitator is offered either control; everyone
  else is told who holds it.
- Selecting cards opens a bar that says how many are selected and why they can
  or cannot be grouped yet. A group is named when it is made.
- A card you add waits in its lane, drawn dashed, until the server has it. If
  the server refuses, the reason is shown and the text is kept.
- The facilitator moves the room through four **stages**: Write, Group, Vote,
  Decide, forward or back. A stage changes what the board puts in front of
  people (the composer in Write, checkboxes in Group, the vote button in Vote,
  the action target in Decide) and **blocks nothing**: a late card, vote or
  action is still accepted in any stage.
- Everything a card can do is in its **menu** (the handle in front of its
  text), which is also the keyboard and touch path for every pointer gesture.
- **Order is shared.** Drag a card by its handle, use Move up / Move down /
  Move to top / Move to bottom / Move to another column in its menu, or press
  `Alt`+`↑`/`↓` (`Shift` for top and bottom). A group moves as one from its own
  menu. **Most votes** re-sorts a column for one reader only and writes
  nothing; while it is on, that column cannot be reordered, and says so. The
  facilitator can apply the vote order for everyone.
- **Stamps.** Anyone can press one of seven stamps (Me too, Thank you, Great
  idea, Quick win, Needs a chat, Blocker, Made me laugh) onto a card from its
  menu. It lands hanging off the card's top edge, clear of the words, and each
  of the twelve a card holds gets a spot of its own; a card with stamps keeps a
  little room above it for them. Whoever pressed it can drag it anywhere on the
  card, nudge it with `Shift`+arrow keys, or remove it with `Delete`; so can
  the facilitator. The arrow keys alone go between a card's stamps, which are
  one Tab stop. **Nobody can see who pressed a stamp**: the server records it
  only to enforce that rule and the limits below, and never publishes it. So
  only a stamp you pressed in this visit looks movable; after a reload you can
  still remove one of yours from its menu, and the server refuses anyone
  else's.
- **An action can come from a card.** The target on a card (or "Start an
  action from this note" in its menu) opens a small form; the action then
  shows the card it came from, and the card shows that it has an action. More
  cards can be tied to an existing action, and untied.
- **A timer** the facilitator can start (1, 3, 5, 10 minutes or a custom
  1–180), pause, extend by a minute, and clear. It is counted on the server's
  clock and published as time remaining. It never blocks anything: when it
  runs out the board says "Time's up" and keeps working. Changing the stage
  clears it.
- Typing is never interrupted: other people's changes update the board in
  place and are announced to screen readers.

CSV export is the host's: it flattens the guest's `on_session_state` document
(`revealed`, `stage`, `timer`, `stamps`, `columns`, `cards`, `groups`,
`actionItems`) next to `id,kind,title,phase`. Authorship that has not been
revealed is not in that document, so it is not in the file; who voted and who
pressed a stamp are never in it.

## Actions and limits

| Action | Who | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `add-card`, `group-cards`, `vote`, `add-action` | anyone in the room | as before; `add-action` also takes `sourceIds`, and a blank `owner` means nobody |
| `delete-card` | the card's author | delete a card with its votes, stamps and links |
| `set-owner`, `delete-action` | anyone | name or clear an action's owner; delete an action |
| `move-card`, `move-group` | anyone | reorder a card or a group, move a card to another column or group |
| `stamp`, `move-stamp`, `remove-stamp` | anyone; move and remove only for the stamp's presser | press, move, lift a stamp |
| `link-action` | anyone | tie a card or group to an action, or untie it |
| `reveal`, `conceal` | facilitator | show or hide authors |
| `set-stage` | facilitator | set the stage, 0 to 3 |
| `timer` | facilitator | `start`, `pause`, `resume`, `add`, `clear` |
| `order-by-votes` | facilitator | reorder one column by votes for everyone |
| `moderate-stamp` | facilitator | move or remove any stamp |
| `moderate-card` | facilitator | delete any card |

Facilitator-only actions are refused by the host before the plugin runs.
Everything else is validated by the guest: unknown ids, text that is empty or
too long, coordinates outside the card, somebody else's card or stamp, and
anything over a limit are **declined** and change nothing. The guest answers
the host with one of four codes (`invalid`, `forbidden`, `not-found`,
`conflict`) instead of failing, so a participant sending nonsense cannot count
against the plugin; that needs a host that understands the answer, and an
older host treats a declined request as accepted with no change.

- A board holds at most **120 cards**, **30 from any one person**, each up to
  **500 characters**; **40 groups**, named in up to 80 characters; **1,000
  votes**; **30 action items** of up to 500 characters, with an owner of up to
  64.
- A card holds at most **12 stamps**, **3 from any one person**; a board holds
  **300**, **60 from any one person**.
- A timer runs from **10 seconds to 3 hours**.
- An action links to at most **12** cards or groups.
- A group is removed when its last card leaves it.

Every board of an org shares the plugin's one storage quota (1 MiB). A board
at every limit at once, written in characters that take three bytes each, is
about 340 KB; an ordinary one is a few tens of kilobytes.

The new actions do not change what the plugin is granted. An upgrade from
0.1.0 asks for the same three grants, so it applies without a new consent.

## Installing it

Take the retrospective's `.parley` bundle (a release asset, signed when the
release key is configured, otherwise named `-UNSIGNED.parley`), trust its signer,
upload it on the plugin catalog page, and install it for an org; see
[Plugins: operate](/operations/plugins/). The grants it asks for are `kv` (scope
`board`), `session:read`, and `session:act`. Consent copy for those comes from
the host, not from this page.

Disable mid-session and the room shows the existing "kind unavailable" state;
enable it again and the kind returns. Uninstall is refused while any
retrospective session still exists. Those two rules are host behavior.

## One document per session

The plugin key-value store can get and set a key. It cannot list keys or scan a
prefix. Grouping and vote tallies therefore live on **one namespaced document**
per room. Writes are read-modify-write and last-write-wins; compare-and-swap is
not on this package.

The UI never fetches. `connect-src 'none'` is the frame's policy; every byte it
sees arrives over the MessageChannel bridge, including design tokens.
